What this site is

Winter Car Prep is a small guide site for drivers who face real winters. Every article answers one question in plain language: what to do, what it costs, and what you can skip.

What we cover

The site is built around five core topics — winter tires, battery care, emergency kits, rust protection and the full winter checklist — plus a blog of 42 articles that tackle the specific mistakes drivers make in cold weather: warming the car up wrong, scraping ice the wrong way, jump-starting with the cables in the wrong order, and dozens more. Each article follows the same shape: what most people do wrong, why it is wrong, and the right way, step by step.

The blog also features the TikTok creators who teach car skills on camera — from the Chinese explainers with millions of followers to a Canadian dealer and a US highway department — because some tricks are easier to learn by watching than by reading.

The free tools

Three tools run right on the site, with no sign-up and no download: the Winter Kit Builder, which builds a personal emergency-kit checklist from three questions; the Will My Car Start Tomorrow? predictor, which checks tomorrow's overnight low against your battery's age; and a cold tire pressure calculator on the winter tires page. All three run in your browser and store nothing.

How the guides are made

Guides are written from general automotive knowledge and public safety guidance, and checked against manufacturer advice where it matters — tire pressures, battery ratings, jump-start sequences. Where a guide quotes a creator or a news report, it says so. Where a video is embedded, it is the creator's own official video. If a fact turns out to be wrong, we fix it and say so — corrections go to the contact page.

How the site makes money

The site is built to earn through advertising and affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate, it earns from qualifying purchases made through links on the site, at no extra cost to the reader. Ad placements are marked as advertisements, and sponsored placements are always labelled "Sponsored". Advertising and sponsorship never change what a guide recommends — a guide's job is to tell you the truth about your car, not to sell you something.

A note on accuracy

Guides are written from general automotive knowledge and public safety guidance. Prices are typical ranges in Canada and the US and change over time. Always confirm fitment, specs and prices for your own vehicle before buying parts. Nothing on this site is a substitute for a qualified mechanic: if your car has a warning light, a strange noise or a safety problem, get it checked in person.
