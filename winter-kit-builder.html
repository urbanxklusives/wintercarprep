Frequently asked questions

What should be in a winter emergency kit?

At minimum: a warm blanket, flashlight, phone charger, jumper cables or jump starter, ice scraper, small shovel, sand or cat litter for traction, reflective triangles, a first-aid kit, water and snacks, and hand warmers. The builder above tailors the list to your winters and passengers.

How much does a winter car kit cost?

Most of it you already own. Buying the rest — blanket, shovel, jump starter, sand — runs about $75–$150. A portable jump starter ($60–$100) is the single best buy on the list.

Where should I keep my emergency kit?

In the trunk, in one bag or box, where you can reach it without unpacking the car. Check it every November — replace dead batteries, stale snacks and frozen water.

Do I need a different kit for highway driving?

Yes — longer trips mean longer waits. Add a reflective vest, a full-size shovel, and extra food and water. Remote driving adds a tow strap and a way to call for help out of cell range.
