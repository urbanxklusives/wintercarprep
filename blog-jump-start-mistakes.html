Jump-Starting a Dead Battery: The Mistakes That Can Fry Your Car

One wrong clamp can fry your car's electronics or spark a battery. The order matters — here's the right one.

Advertisement

Photo: Paul Sequeira (U.S. National Archives), Public Domain, via Wikimedia Commons

A dead battery on a cold morning is stressful enough without making it worse. But most people jump-starting a car get at least one thing wrong: the order of the cables, where the last clamp goes, or how long they leave it connected. Get the order wrong and you can send a voltage spike through your car's electronics, spark a battery, or melt a cable. Jump-starting is safe and simple — but only if you follow the sequence.

Why it's wrong

The classic mistake is clamping both cables straight onto both batteries, black to black, in any order. That last clamp, made right at the dead battery, can spark — and a charging or recently charged battery gives off hydrogen gas. A spark near hydrogen is how batteries explode. Reversing the cables is the expensive mistake: positive to negative sends current backwards through the car's electrical system and can fry the alternator, the computer, or both in seconds. And rushing the job — starting the dead car the instant the cables are on — doesn't give the dead battery any time to take a charge, so you grind the starter and drain the good battery too.

The right way

  1. Check the battery first. If the case is cracked, swollen, or leaking, or the battery is frozen solid, do not jump it. A damaged battery can burst. Call for a tow instead.
  2. Red to dead. Clamp the red (+) cable to the dead battery's positive terminal.
  3. Red to good. Clamp the other red (+) cable to the good battery's positive terminal.
  4. Black to good. Clamp the black (−) cable to the good battery's negative terminal.
  5. Black to bare metal. Clamp the last black cable to a bare, unpainted metal bolt or bracket on the dead car's engine — away from the battery. This is the step that keeps the spark away from the battery.
  6. Start the good car and wait a minute or two. Let the dead battery take a charge before you try the starter.
  7. Start the dead car. If it doesn't start in a few seconds, wait another minute and try again. Don't grind the starter.
  8. Remove the cables in reverse order. Black from the metal, black from the good battery, red from the good battery, red from the dead battery.
  9. Drive for at least 20 to 30 minutes. The alternator needs real driving time to recharge the battery. A five-minute trip won't do it.

The rundown

Red to dead, red to good, black to good, black to bare metal — and reverse to remove. Never clamp the last cable to the dead battery, never reverse the colours, and never jump a damaged or frozen battery. If your car is a hybrid or electric, check your owner's manual first — some have a special jump point under the hood, and some should not be jump-started from another car at all.
