Winter Emergency Kit: 15 Things to Keep in Your Car

A breakdown in winter is a different animal. This kit turns it into an inconvenience instead of an emergency.

Advertisement

The kit

  1. Warm blanket (or sleeping bag)
  2. Extra gloves, hat and socks
  3. Flashlight + spare batteries
  4. Phone charger (car + portable battery)
  5. Jumper cables or a portable jump starter
  6. Ice scraper + snow brush
  7. Small folding shovel
  8. Bag of sand or cat litter (traction)
  9. Water bottles (2) and granola bars
  10. First-aid kit
  11. Reflective triangles or flares
  12. Basic tools + tire pressure gauge
  13. Windshield washer fluid (winter formula)
  14. Hand warmers
  15. A paper list of emergency numbers

What it costs

Most of it you already own. Buying the rest — blanket, shovel, jump starter, sand — runs about $75–$150 total. A portable jump starter ($60–$100) is the single best buy on the list: it starts your car with no second vehicle needed.

Where to keep it

In the trunk, in one bag or box, where you can reach it without unpacking the car. Check it every November — replace dead batteries, stale snacks and frozen water.

If you get stuck

Stay with your car. Run the engine 10–15 minutes per hour for heat, crack a window, and clear the exhaust pipe of snow first — a blocked exhaust pushes carbon monoxide into the cabin.
